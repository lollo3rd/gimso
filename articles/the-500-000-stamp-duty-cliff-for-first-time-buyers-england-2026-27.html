The £500,000 Stamp Duty cliff for first-time buyers (England, 2026/27)

November 26, 2026

If you are buying your first home in England or Northern Ireland, Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) has a cliff edge that matters most in London, where so many flats sit right around £500,000.

The rule

First-time buyer relief, as published on GOV.UK:

  • 0% on the first £300,000
  • 5% on the portion from £300,001 to £500,000
  • If the price is above £500,000, no relief at all — you pay standard rates on the whole price: 0% up to £125,000, 2% from £125,001 to £250,000, 5% from £250,001 to £925,000, 10% to £1.5m, 12% above.

Relief is not tapered. It switches off.

What it means in practice

1. Negotiating near £500k is worth more than it looks. Getting a £510,000 asking price down to £500,000 saves £10,000 on the price and £5,500 of Stamp Duty (£15,500 → £10,000).

2. Stamp Duty comes out of your cash, not your mortgage. On a £550,000 flat with 15% down (£82,500), the £17,500 of SDLT is about a fifth of the deposit again — money that has to be saved on top.

3. It changes the rent-vs-buy answer. That cash would otherwise stay invested. If you are planning for financial independence, Stamp Duty is not a one-off fee to forget: it is money that stops compounding, and it should sit in any honest comparison between buying and renting-and-investing.

Not covered here

  • Scotland (LBTT) and Wales (LTT) have their own rules.
  • Additional properties pay +5% and non-UK residents +2%.
  • Rates are those in force for 2026/27; check GOV.UK before you exchange, since a Budget can change them.
